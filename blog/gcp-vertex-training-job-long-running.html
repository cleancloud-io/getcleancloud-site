The Fine-Tune That Wouldn't Stop

The team was fine-tuning a large language model for a domain-specific task. The training configuration had worked before — 3 epochs on a curated dataset, historically completing in 16–18 hours on an 8× A100 node. They submitted the job to Vertex AI, set up a Slack webhook for job completion, and went to sleep.

The Slack notification never came. The job was still running the next morning. And the morning after that. A data pipeline change had introduced an infinite repeat loop in the dataset iterator — the model was training on the same data indefinitely without ever completing an epoch. Because there was no timeout configured and the job was technically making forward progress (loss was decreasing, slowly), Vertex AI had no reason to stop it.

At $80/hour for 8× A100 compute, 43 extra hours cost $3,440 in excess spend beyond the expected job cost. Total waste: $4,880 — more than the original job budget.

Vertex AI Custom Jobs do not have default timeouts. Without an explicit timeout in the job spec, a Custom Job runs until it completes, fails, or is manually cancelled. A stalled or runaway job accumulates compute cost indefinitely on GPU/TPU hardware.

Why Training Jobs Exceed Expected Duration

Training jobs are the highest-cost-per-hour AI/ML workload in any cloud environment. GPU and TPU nodes bill at rates that can exceed $80/hour per node. When a job runs significantly longer than expected, the cost impact is immediate and compounding.

Common causes of runaway training jobs on Vertex AI:

  • Data pipeline bugs: An infinite loop or repeated dataset with no epoch boundary. The job processes data forever, making incremental progress on a never-ending dataset.
  • Convergence failure: A learning rate issue, gradient explosion, or initialization problem causes loss to oscillate rather than converge. The job continues running but is not making useful progress.
  • Larger-than-expected dataset: A data pipeline pulls more records than anticipated — perhaps a filter condition was removed or a staging dataset grew. The job finishes, but takes 3× longer than planned.
  • Distributed training imbalance: One worker node in a multi-node distributed job is slower than others due to hardware variability or a data skew. The fast workers wait for the slow one, stretching wall-clock time.
  • Missing early stopping: The model reaches a useful quality threshold after 20% of the planned epochs, but training continues through all planned epochs with diminishing returns.

How the Detection Rule Works

CleanCloud's gcp.vertex.training_job.long_running rule monitors both Vertex AI Custom Jobs and Training Pipelines. It flags jobs that have been running longer than a configurable threshold (default: 24 hours) and provides an early warning for GPU/TPU jobs approaching that threshold.

What's covered

  • Custom Jobs (projects/{project}/locations/{location}/customJobs): Direct training jobs submitted via the Vertex AI API or SDK.
  • Training Pipelines (projects/{project}/locations/{location}/trainingPipelines): Managed training workflows including AutoML and custom container pipelines.

Detection logic

  1. State filter: Only jobs in JOB_STATE_RUNNING (Custom Jobs) or PIPELINE_STATE_RUNNING (Training Pipelines) are evaluated. Queued, completed, failed, and cancelled jobs are excluded.
  2. Duration check: The rule calculates now - createTime (wall-clock elapsed time). Jobs exceeding the max_duration_hours threshold (default: 24 hours) are flagged.
  3. GPU/TPU early warning: For jobs using GPU or TPU accelerators, a warning flag is issued at 90% of the threshold duration. A job configured with A100 GPUs and a 24-hour threshold receives an early warning at ~21.6 hours — before the threshold is crossed, giving teams time to inspect and cancel if needed.
  4. Severity ratio: duration_ratio = elapsed_hours / threshold_hours. A job running 3× the threshold has a ratio of 3.0 and is flagged HIGH confidence.

Confidence and risk tiers:

  • HIGH confidence: Job has been running for at least 3× the threshold duration (duration_ratio ≥ 3.0).
  • MEDIUM confidence: Job has exceeded the threshold but duration_ratio < 3.0.
  • CRITICAL risk: HIGH confidence + GPU/TPU accelerators. A GPU job running 3× its expected duration on expensive hardware.
  • HIGH risk: HIGH confidence, no GPU/TPU (CPU-only long-running job).
  • MEDIUM risk: MEDIUM confidence, any hardware configuration.

Early warning is GPU/TPU-specific. The 90% early warning flag is issued only for jobs using GPU or TPU accelerators. CPU-only jobs are not flagged until they cross the threshold, as CPU training jobs are lower-cost and more likely to have legitimately long runtimes for large-scale data processing tasks.

Catching It Before It Becomes a Budget Crisis

The 90% early warning is the key differentiator. By the time a GPU job on 8× A100 hardware has consumed 90% of its expected duration, your team gets a flag. If everything looks normal — loss is converging, workers are healthy — you can dismiss it. If the job looks stalled, you can cancel before crossing into excess spend territory.

.github/workflows/cleancloud-gcp.ymlYAML
name: CleanCloud GCP AI/ML Scan
on:
  schedule:
    - cron: '0 * * * *'  # Hourly — important for active training jobs
  workflow_dispatch:

jobs:
  cleancloud-scan:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4

      - name: GCP authentication
        uses: google-github-actions/auth@v2
        with:
          credentials_json: ${{ secrets.GCP_CREDENTIALS }}

      - name: CleanCloud — GCP AI/ML scan
        uses: cleancloud-io/scan-action@v1
        with:
          provider: gcp
          category: ai
          fail-on-confidence: HIGH

For training job monitoring, consider running scans hourly rather than weekly. GPU training jobs can accumulate significant excess cost within hours. The early warning at 90% of threshold gives you a narrow window to intervene — an hourly scan maximizes the chance of catching it in time.

Using Vertex AI Native Timeouts

CleanCloud's rule provides detection and alerting. For prevention, use Vertex AI's built-in timeout mechanism:

Vertex AI Custom Job with timeout (Python SDK)PYTHON
from google.cloud import aiplatform
from google.protobuf import duration_pb2

job = aiplatform.CustomContainerTrainingJob(
    display_name="llm-finetune-job",
    container_uri="us-docker.pkg.dev/my-project/my-repo/trainer:latest",
)

job.run(
    machine_type="a2-megagpu-8g",
    accelerator_type="NVIDIA_TESLA_A100",
    accelerator_count=8,
    # Set timeout to 20 hours — 2h buffer above 18h expected
    timeout=duration_pb2.Duration(seconds=72000),  # 20h = 72000s
)

With a timeout set, Vertex AI will automatically cancel the job and terminate the compute at the configured duration — even if the job is still running. CleanCloud's early warning at 90% of the threshold gives you a CI alert before the timeout fires, allowing a human to inspect the job state and decide whether to let the timeout apply or cancel early.

Preventing Runaway Training Jobs

Detection catches the problem in progress. Prevention stops it from happening:

  • Always set a timeout: Every Custom Job and Training Pipeline should have an explicit timeout. Use your expected duration plus a 20–30% buffer. A job that consistently needs more time than the buffer signals a problem worth investigating.
  • Validate dataset sizes before submission: Run a quick count of records in the training data pipeline before submitting multi-hour GPU jobs. Catch the infinite loop or unexpected dataset growth before the job starts.
  • Implement training callbacks with early stopping: Configure early stopping based on validation loss plateau. A job that isn't converging after N epochs of no improvement should stop itself.
  • Monitor GPU utilization, not just job state: A job in JOB_STATE_RUNNING with 5% GPU utilization (data pipeline bottleneck) is effectively wasted spend. Cloud Monitoring can alert on low GPU utilization for running custom jobs.
  • Run CleanCloud hourly for active training periods: During sprints with active GPU training, hourly scans maximize the chance of catching issues before they compound.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Vertex AI automatically stop long-running training jobs?

Only if you configure a timeout. Without an explicit timeout in the Custom Job spec, Vertex AI runs the job until it completes, fails with an error, or is manually cancelled. The platform has no built-in maximum job duration.

What is the 90% early warning based on?

The early warning is issued when a GPU or TPU job's elapsed wall-clock time reaches 90% of the configured max_duration_hours threshold (default: 24 hours). At 90%, the job has not yet exceeded the threshold and may legitimately be close to completion — the flag is informational, not a definitive problem signal. It gives teams a window to check before the threshold is crossed and a full finding is generated.

Does this rule cover Vertex AI Pipelines (Kubeflow/Managed Pipelines)?

The gcp.vertex.training_job.long_running rule covers Vertex AI Custom Jobs and Vertex AI Training Pipelines (the managed training-specific pipeline API). It does not cover general Vertex AI Pipelines (the Kubeflow-based workflow orchestration API), which have different use cases and billing models.

How is this different from the SageMaker training job rule?

The Vertex AI rule includes the 90% early warning for GPU/TPU jobs. The AWS SageMaker rule (aws.sagemaker.training_job.long_running) does not have an early warning mechanism — it flags jobs only after they have exceeded the configured stopping condition or duration threshold. The early warning is a GCP-specific capability in CleanCloud.
