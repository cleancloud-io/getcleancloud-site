Runaway SageMaker Training Jobs:
How a Hung GPU Job Can Cost $2,000 a Day

A SageMaker training job that gets stuck — waiting for data, deadlocked in distributed synchronization, or simply misconfigured — keeps billing GPU instances at full on-demand rates until someone manually stops it. The meter doesn't stop because the job stopped making progress.

S
Suresh · CleanCloud
· April 22, 2026 · 8 min read

The Problem With Long-Running Training Jobs

Unlike SageMaker endpoints (which are meant to run continuously), training jobs are expected to finish. A well-behaved training job runs for a predictable duration, writes its model artifacts to S3, and terminates. The billing stops when the job terminates.

The problem is that jobs don't always terminate cleanly. A job can get stuck in InProgress state for many reasons, and SageMaker has no default timeout configured — by default, a training job will run forever unless you set a stopping condition.

Common causes of runaway training jobs:

  • Data pipeline stall: A distributed training job hangs waiting for a slow or failed data worker to catch up. The job is technically still running — no errors, no failures — but making zero forward progress.
  • Distributed training deadlock: In multi-node jobs using AllReduce or AllGather operations (PyTorch DDP, Horovod), a single failed rank can cause the entire job to hang indefinitely waiting for gradient synchronization.
  • Evaluation loop hang: A custom evaluation callback hits a resource that becomes unavailable (database, external API, mounted EFS), blocking the training loop without raising an exception.
  • Checkpoint write failure: The job finishes training but hangs trying to write a checkpoint to S3, often due to a permissions issue or network partition.
  • Developer-left job: A data scientist started a job for experimentation, saw intermediate results they liked, and moved on to other work — without realizing the job was still running and billing.

SageMaker's default behavior: No timeout. A job with no StoppingCondition.MaxRuntimeInSeconds will run indefinitely. This is the default for new training jobs unless you explicitly configure it.

How CleanCloud Detects Runaway Jobs

The aws.sagemaker.training_job.long_running rule checks for training jobs that remain in InProgress state beyond the configured threshold (default: 24 hours):

  1. Lists in-progress jobs: Queries sagemaker:ListTrainingJobs filtered to InProgress status.
  2. Measures elapsed time: Uses TrainingStartTime when available (the time instances were actually provisioned and training began), falling back to CreationTime when TrainingStartTime isn't yet set.
  3. Classifies by duration and instance type: GPU-backed jobs beyond the threshold are flagged HIGH risk. CPU-backed jobs are flagged MEDIUM risk. Confidence is HIGH when the job has exceeded a configured SageMaker stopping-condition limit, MEDIUM otherwise.

The rule is opt-in via --category ai. It runs alongside idle endpoint and notebook detection when you use --category ai or --category all.

Configuring the Time Threshold

The default 24-hour threshold works well for most teams. If your training jobs routinely take longer — multi-day pretraining runs, for example — you can adjust the threshold per-rule in cleancloud.yaml:

cleancloud.yaml — custom threshold adjust for long training runs 
rules:
  aws.sagemaker.training_job.long_running:
    max_runtime_hours: 72   # 3 days — adjust for your team's typical job duration

Setting a per-rule threshold does not affect other rules and does not change how findings are reported — it only adjusts when a job crosses the threshold and becomes a finding.

Prevention: Configure Stopping Conditions

The most reliable way to prevent runaway training cost is to configure SageMaker's built-in stopping condition on every training job. This sets a hard maximum runtime after which SageMaker automatically terminates the job:

Python SDK — set a stopping condition prevents runaway billing 
from sagemaker.estimator import Estimator

estimator = Estimator(
    image_uri=training_image,
    instance_type='ml.p3.8xlarge',
    instance_count=4,
    # Hard stop after 6 hours — prevents runaway billing
    max_run=6 * 60 * 60,  # seconds
    max_wait=7 * 60 * 60,  # queue wait time (for spot instances)
)

CleanCloud flags jobs that don't have a stopping condition (or have one set far beyond typical job durations) as a governance finding. Detection catches what prevention misses — jobs where the stopping condition was set but too high, or where it was accidentally omitted on an experimental run.

Setting Up Automated Detection

For a team running regular training experiments, a daily scan (not just weekly) makes sense — a runaway job detected on day two costs far less than one caught after a week:

.github/workflows/sagemaker-training-watch.yml Daily scan — catches runaway jobs within 24 hours 
name: SageMaker training job watch
on:
  schedule:
    - cron: '0 */12 * * *'   # Twice daily

jobs:
  scan:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    permissions:
      id-token: write
      contents: read
    steps:
      - uses: aws-actions/configure-aws-credentials@v4
        with:
          role-to-assume: arn:aws:iam::${{ vars.AWS_ACCOUNT_ID }}:role/CleanCloudCIReadOnly
          aws-region: us-east-1

      - uses: cleancloud-io/scan-action@v1
        with:
          provider: aws
          all-regions: 'true'
          category: ai
          fail-on-confidence: HIGH
          output: json
          output-file: training-findings.json
          artifact-name: training-watch-report

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SageMaker bill while a training job is stuck in InProgress?

Yes. Billing is based on instance uptime from job start to job termination. A job stuck in InProgress — making zero progress but not raising any error — bills at the same rate as a healthy, actively training job.

How do I stop a runaway SageMaker training job?

Call sagemaker:StopTrainingJob with the job name. In the console: navigate to SageMaker Training Jobs, select the job, and click Stop. Billing stops when the job transitions to Stopping and then Stopped.

What's the difference between max_run and a stopping condition?

They're the same thing expressed differently. max_run in the SageMaker Python SDK sets StoppingCondition.MaxRuntimeInSeconds in the API. Both cause SageMaker to automatically stop the job after the specified number of seconds regardless of training progress.
